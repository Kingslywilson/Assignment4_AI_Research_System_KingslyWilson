Climate Change and Health

Climate change is a major threat to human health. Rising temperatures,
extreme weather events, changing rainfall patterns, and other climate-related
changes can affect people directly and indirectly.

Extreme heat can cause heat exhaustion, heatstroke, cardiovascular problems,
and other health complications. Older adults, children, outdoor workers, and
people with existing health vulnerabilities can be particularly affected.

Climate change can also influence infectious diseases. Changes in temperature
and rainfall can affect the distribution of mosquitoes and other disease
vectors. This can increase the risk of diseases such as malaria and dengue
in some regions.

Food and water security are also affected by climate change. Droughts,
flooding, and changing agricultural conditions can reduce food availability
and affect access to safe drinking water.

Climate change can also affect mental health. Extreme weather events,
displacement, loss of homes, and disruption of communities can contribute
to stress, anxiety, and other psychological difficulties.

Health systems need to prepare for climate-related risks. Climate-resilient
health infrastructure, disease surveillance, emergency preparedness, and
public-health planning can help communities respond to changing conditions.

Reducing greenhouse-gas emissions can provide health benefits. Cleaner
energy and reduced air pollution can improve air quality while also helping
to limit long-term climate change.

Important evidence gaps remain. These include differences in climate impacts
between regions, long-term mental-health effects, the resilience of health
systems, and the full economic consequences of climate-related health risks.
